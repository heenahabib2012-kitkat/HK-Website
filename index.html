01 — Origin

Behind every leader is a story that starts somewhere small. Habib Koya’s starts in a small town in Karnataka, in a house run by his mother — a housewife who kept cattle, cooked in large batches to support their family and local factory workers, and turned her home into a hub of quiet entrepreneurial energy.

From the age of seven, Habib helped her — sticking posters, watching her manage work with grace, absorbing the grit and resourcefulness of someone building something out of very little. His love of food, people, and hustle was born in those smoke-filled kitchens, long before he had a name for any of it.

In the early 1990s his curiosity moved beyond home. Fascinated by his brother’s engineering work on the Konkan Railway tunnel through the Western Ghats, he joined Universal Engineering Company as a trainee. Years of on-ground learning and problem-solving followed — the same qualities that, decades later, would make him the company’s Executive Director.

01

Auntie Anne’s
in the Middle East

Starbucks Kuwait → Auntie Anne’s, 2005–2009

  1. Problem
  2. Decision
  3. Evidence
  4. Result

The problem

As District Manager at Starbucks Coffee, Kuwait, Koya’s dedication caught the attention of a regular customer — an encounter that could easily have stayed a pleasant conversation. Instead it became a proposition: bring an entirely new American brand into a region that had never seen it, with no local playbook, no local team, and no local precedent to follow.

The decision

“If you join me, I’ll bring a brand from America for you to build.”

Koya said yes. Rather than run the venture from Kuwait, he travelled to the United States himself to train directly with the brand — choosing to learn the operation first-hand before trying to build it somewhere it had never existed.

The evidence

He returned from the U.S. having trained on the brand’s own operating model, then built the Middle East presence from the ground up — sourcing, training, and opening store by store rather than franchising the problem away.

The result

Auntie Anne’s opened seven successful stores across Kuwait and Iraq — a new-to-region brand launch executed personally, with no existing template to lean on.

02

Van Cleef & Arpels
arrives in Kuwait

Hussein Morad Bebehani Group, Commercial Director

  1. Problem
  2. Decision
  3. Evidence
  4. Result

The problem

By 2009, Koya’s performance as General Manager of Costa Coffee had caught the attention of Hussein Morad Bebehani, a Kuwaiti business tycoon, who appointed him Commercial Director of the entire Hussein Morad Bebehani Group. The mandate: bring globally exclusive luxury houses into a market with no existing relationship to them.

The decision

Rather than negotiate at a distance, Koya flew to Paris to pitch directly to the President of Van Cleef & Arpels — making the case for Kuwait himself, in person, for a house that answers to very few outside markets.

The evidence

The pitch secured exclusive rights to Van Cleef & Arpels for Kuwait — a feat few could achieve — alongside bringing Costa Coffee and Bvlgari into the same market under his leadership.

The result

The Group’s portfolio extended into fashion, luxury watches, and jewellery, leaving a lasting mark on the region’s luxury retail landscape — built on a single in-person pitch rather than an intermediary deal.

03

Building HK
Consultancy

Founded 2016 (Relax Business Solutions) — relaunched 2021

  1. Problem
  2. Decision
  3. Evidence
  4. Result

The problem

Long before it was a business, this was a habit: friends and founders sitting down with Koya over coffee, mapping out their businesses on napkins and phone notes. Many of those casual sessions quietly turned into multi-million-dollar successes — but the advice itself had nowhere formal to live.

The decision

In 2016, reflecting on a career that traced back to his mother’s own entrepreneurial spirit, Koya founded Relax Business Solutions. In 2021 he formalized the practice further, launching HK Consultancy — built to turn three decades of global operating experience into structured, personal support for entrepreneurs.

The evidence

The consultancy draws directly on experience across 43 countries and teams from 18+ nationalities — a working knowledge of global cultures, business psychology, and operations that most advisors only theorize about.

The result

HK Consultancy now stands as a boutique incubator for retail, F&B, and lifestyle brands across the Middle East and India — the same instinct his mother modeled in her kitchen, formalized into a practice.
